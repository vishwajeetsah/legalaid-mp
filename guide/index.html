Know your rights

Free legal aid is a right, not a favour. Here is, in simple words, who can get it, what it includes, what happens after you apply, and how to get help even before an arrest.

Who can get free legal aid

Under Section 12 of the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, the following persons are entitled to free legal aid whatever their income:

  • A womanSection 12(c)
  • A child (under 18 years)Section 12(c)
  • Member of SC / ST communitySection 12(a)
  • Industrial workmanSection 12(f)
  • Currently in custodySection 12(g)
  • Victim of human trafficking or begar (forced labour)Section 12(b)
  • A person with disability or mental illnessSection 12(d)
  • Victim of a disaster, caste atrocity or violenceSection 12(e)
  • Any other person whose annual family income is below the limit prescribed by their State Government (Section 12(h))Section 12(h)

What you get — completely free

  • A lawyer for your case

    An advocate will represent you in court, tribunal or before any authority — you pay nothing for this.

  • Pleadings and appeals prepared for you

    Preparation of pleadings, memo of appeal and paper book — including printing and translation of documents.

  • Drafting of legal documents

    Drafting of legal documents, petitions and applications, including special leave petitions.

  • Everything needed to conduct your case

    Any service required in the conduct of your case or legal proceeding before any court, tribunal or authority.

  • Free legal advice

    Advice on any legal matter — even if you do not have a court case.

  • Help with government welfare schemes

    Aid and advice to access benefits under welfare laws and schemes of the Central and State Governments.

What happens after you apply

  1. 1

    Legal aid is provided through a chain of authorities working from the national level down to your taluka: NALSA (national), the State Legal Services Authority, your District Legal Services Authority (DLSA) and the Taluk Legal Services Committee.

  2. 2

    Submit your application at your district's DLSA office. The office checks your eligibility and documents, and assigns a panel lawyer to you free of cost.

  3. 3

    Applied at the wrong office? Do not worry — an application received by NALSA or any other authority is forwarded to the concerned authority. It is not rejected for being filed at the wrong place.

  4. 4

    For matters before the Supreme Court, the Supreme Court Legal Services Committee provides legal aid; for the High Court, the High Court Legal Services Committee does.

Help before arrest — do not wait

You do not have to wait to be arrested to get legal help. If the police have sent you a notice asking you to appear, or you fear you may be arrested, you can approach your DLSA for free pre-arrest legal assistance. A lawyer can advise you, help you respond to the notice and appear with you.

Keep these details ready when you ask for pre-arrest help

  • •FIR number and year (if an FIR has been registered)
  • •Name of the police station
  • •The police notice and its date, and the date you must appear
  • •The district / place where arrest is apprehended
  • •Your mobile number and the name & number of a family member or other contact person
  • •A short summary of the facts of the case
